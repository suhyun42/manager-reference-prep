01 / Role context

What OpenAI is hiring for

A strategic finance individual contributor supporting the API Platform and other B2B products. They want someone who understands pricing, commitment offers, revenue modeling, and unit economics and can turn those concepts into practical business recommendations.

Business judgment

Connect growth to economics

Evaluate adoption, commercial terms, customer usage, revenue, compute costs, and margin together.

Independent ownership

Structure ambiguous decisions

Frame the problem, develop an analysis, recommend a direction, and bring stakeholders along.

Cross-functional influence

Move decisions into execution

Navigate competing incentives and help Product, Finance, and GTM reach implementable decisions.

In the interview discussions, Jacob emphasized autonomy, sound business judgment, and bringing structure to noisy or ambiguous problems.

02 / Background & motivation

My background & why this role

I'm a Senior Business Planner at Microsoft, leading pricing, packaging, and product economics across the Data and AI portfolio. I've been at Microsoft for about four years, with the last three on the Monetization and Business Planning team.

My work combines strategic decisions—growth, retention, monetization, business cases—with operational execution, including launch implementation, billing meters, and post-launch questions. I partner with Product, Engineering, Finance, Sales, Marketing, and Legal, and present recommendations to senior leadership depending on impact.

Before Microsoft, I spent roughly five years in growth and strategy, focused on customer research and experimentation. I completed my MBA at Cornell to move closer to implementation and financial decision-making.

Why OpenAI: I want deeper ownership of the financial decisions behind scaling B2B products—especially margin tradeoffs, growth investments, and where leadership places bets.

to owning the portfolio

06 / Development

How she developed with experience

These are development themes and progress to reflect on, rather than assumed current weaknesses. Please ground any feedback in what you personally observed.

Building operational foresight

As she gained more experience, Alicia became better at anticipating lead times and bottlenecks. She learned to prepare operational requirements earlier, find practical workarounds, and be explicit about where management support was needed.

Keeping disagreement in perspective

Alicia learned not to take stakeholder disagreement personally and appeared less stressed with more repetitions and experience. She recognized that it is business: GTM and PM may prioritize adoption, while Finance focuses on protecting the business and margins. Understanding those incentives helped her navigate tension more calmly.
